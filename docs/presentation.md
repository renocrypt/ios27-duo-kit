# Duo — presentation brief

Status: direction, version 0.1, September 23, 2026. Written for `app/`'s next milestones ("per-posture camera choreography" and "the stage's visual direction," `app/README.md` Status). Grounds Chrome-measured Apple data, described industry examples, and our own inferences — each is labeled. Screenshots and page captures opened during research sit in `/tmp/duo/presentation-research/`, disposable; no Apple asset enters this repository.

## Implementation notes (September 23, 2026)

Version 1 is built in `app/src/journey/`; the scenes are data in `scenes.ts`. It departs from this brief in these ways, each for a reason:

- **Scroll length:** 140vh per scene, 230vh for the dawn, instead of 220vh. The scenes change at cuts or with a device move, not by scrubbing, so a long hold only adds scrolling.
- **Posture yaw:** moved from the postures to the camera's azimuth. The posture places the device; the shot chooses the view.
- **Explore moment (scene 5):** the viewer turns the device by hand; the camera does not orbit. The framing and the lens shift that keep the copy clear survive the interaction, and the device springs back when the scene ends.
- **Scene 1 dawn:** passes through a blue hour (the `twilight` mood). Blending night straight into dawn went through grey.
- **Copy:** some body lines were rewritten to tie scenes together. Scene 2 is "Seven things held until nine. One worth an answer now." Scene 4 points at the Touch ID side button, which the screen marks at its real position on the frame.
- **Replay (scene 8):** the restart affordance is a "Start again" pill.
- **Framing:** `fit()` adapts each shot to the viewport: the lens shift (`at`) and a width limit keep the device clear of the copy down to 1024 × 768.
- **Light (principle 3):** each mood's studio is built from its own backdrop. The device reflects the set behind it (the colours the viewer sees) and a dim studio behind the camera, with a black flag at the lens; the studio turns with the camera, as the backdrop does. Dark scenes therefore show black screens with thin edge highlights, as product photography on a dark set does, instead of a white room's veil.
- **Inner display:** off while the device is closed and waking as it opens past 8°, as a foldable's panel does; the closed scenes show only the outer display.

## Design direction

**The feeling: an instrument being tuned, not a phone being sold.** Duo's own thesis is that the hinge is an attention dial — closed is a glance, open is work, set down is ambient (`docs/hub.md` Thesis). The presentation should perform that same dial with its own instruments: the camera holds still and close for a glance, opens its distance and holds longer for work, and drifts to a slow ambient orbit when the device is set down. Camera behavior mirrors device behavior. Every scene answers "how much attention is this getting right now" before it answers "what app is this."

This rejects two easy defaults. It is not a spec-sheet product page (Apple's own `apple.com/iphone-duo/` is that, correctly, for shopping) — our prototype is a *journey*, one continuous day, so pacing is closer to a keynote demo film than a scrolling brochure. And it is not a lab tool: the fold-angle slider and posture buttons that exist today for our own tuning (`app/README.md`) must never appear in the presentation itself. A tool and a story are different screens.

## Principles

**1. Framing and scale.** Measured on `apple.com/iphone-duo/` at a 1440×862 viewport, Chrome 154, 2026-09-23: the hero device (video, `hero/large_2x.mp4`) is 822×545px — 57% of viewport width, 63% of height. In the "Take a closer look" viewer (`#product-viewer`), the device again runs to roughly 65% of viewport height against a plain white ground, alone, with nothing else in frame. Apple never lets the product read as a small object in a big empty field. **Rule: the device fills 50–70% of the frame's short axis in every settled shot.** Our current stage — small, off-centre, on a flat gray field — is the failure mode this rule exists to prevent.

**2. Camera language.** Apple's September 9, 2026 keynote reveal camera pulled back from an extreme macro (an ant on sand) across landscapes before the device appeared (`9to5google`, `Engadget` coverage) — scale is established by contrast and motion, not by a static wide shot. On the page itself, the device generally *holds its pose* while the camera or the crop changes: the interactive viewer swaps between eight fixed angles (Foldable design, Landscape, Portrait, Closed, Seated, Standing, Durability — note four of these are our own posture names) rather than tweening a free orbit. Award-winning WebGL product work agrees on restraint: Lusion's 2026 "cork coaster" launch (Awwwards Site of the Month, April 2026) moves the *camera* through true Z-depth on scroll rather than sliding 2D layers, and is praised specifically because one object gets one camera doing one clear thing per beat. **Rule: pick one mover per beat.** Either the device changes (a fold, a pick-up, a set-down — animated with `motion.spring.hinge` per `app/tokens/motion.tokens.json`) or the camera changes (a dolly, a small arc, a rack focus) — not both at once, or the shot reads as a scramble.

**3. Light and colour per section.** Measured backgrounds on the same Apple page: white by default, `rgb(245,245,247)` ("background-alt") for alternating sections, a dark blue-gray `rgb(57,68,82)` behind the foldable-design/hinge story, and near-black `rgb(29,29,31)` behind the macro camera-detail shots. No two adjacent sections share a background. Our `app/tokens/stage.tokens.json` today holds one flat `#ECECEE` studio for every posture, marked `PROVISIONAL` — that flatness is why nothing feels like it moves through a day. **Rule: background and key-light both change at every scene cut**, tied to the story's actual light (dawn, office daylight, a warm kitchen counter, a dim bedside) rather than staying a neutral product-shot gray throughout.

**4. Typography.** Measured on the same page, same viewport: the hero line "Hello, hello." is 80px / weight 600 / line-height 84px / tracking −1.2px (SF Pro Display); a section headline ("Get the highlights.", "Take a closer look.") is 48px / 600 / lh 52px / tracking −0.144px; a section kicker ("Foldable design", "Versatility") is 25.5px / 600 / lh 37.5px (this one is fluid — likely a `clamp()`, so 25.5px is this viewport's resolved value, not a fixed design size); body copy is 17px / 400. This is a *presentation* scale, distinct from the in-device Dynamic Type scale already tokenized in `app/tokens/typography.tokens.json` (`largeTitle` 34px is the biggest UI text token — too small and too UI-flavored for a stage headline). **Rule: stage copy never borrows UI type sizes.** Headlines run 3–6 words; body runs one short sentence, in Apple's register (short declaratives, a colon or an em dash for the turn: "Foldable design. A new iPhone enters the fold.").

**5. Motion — timing, easing, and who moves.** `app/tokens/motion.tokens.json` already has the right vocabulary: `spring.hinge` (bounce 0, 900ms, "damped, unhurried," per the physical-reference note) for fold changes, `spring.glance` (bounce 0.1, 350ms) for quick outer-display responses, and `duration.ambient` (2000ms ceiling) for anything idle. Apple's page mostly holds the device still and lets crops/tabs swap, or scrubs a captured video — cheap in principle, but the *feel* it protects is worth keeping: nothing moves that doesn't have a reason to. **Rule: reuse these tokens instead of inventing new eases** — hinge changes always use `spring.hinge`; camera moves between scenes use a comparable settle (see tokens below); ambient scenes (Standing, Seated) never loop, matching the hub-brief's own "calm surfaces" rule (`docs/hub.md` Attention rules).

**6. Inviting interaction.** UX research on 3D product viewers is consistent: an interactive model with no visible affordance reads as an unusually heavy static image — the fix is a small, self-cancelling hint (an auto-rotate that stops the moment the person drags, a rotation arrow, a drag cursor) (general UX findings, cited in Sources). Apple's own page separates *story* (scroll, no controls) from *inspection* ("Take a closer look," an explicit tabbed viewer with its own affordances) rather than blending them. Our `Stage` class already has `OrbitControls` wired in (`app/src/scene/Stage.ts`) — that is the hook. **Rule: the journey scrolls hands-off; a single, clearly separate "explore" moment (after the story, or the Book scene where two panes invite comparison) is where dragging is offered, with one small hint that disappears after first use.**

**7. What to avoid.** A gray, unchanging background (measured failure vs. principle 3). A debug button panel or slider left visible (this is a tool, not a scene — see Design direction). A small, off-centre device (violates principle 1's 50–70% rule). Wide-angle-lens framing, which distorts the frame's flat glass and cheapens it — Apple's product photography and our own narrow default camera (`Stage.ts` sets `PerspectiveCamera(22, …)`, a long, flattening lens) should stay in that family; do not widen it for a "dramatic" establishing shot. Stacking effects (bloom *and* chromatic aberration *and* film grain *and* parallax) — award juries explicitly mark this down; "beauty that drops frames doesn't place" (Awwwards/FWA commentary, cited in Sources). Looping ambient motion, which the hub-brief already rejects for the device UI and which reads as cheap for the stage too.

## Storyboard: a day with Duo

Nine scenes, extending the journey already outlined in `docs/hub.md` ("Prototype direction") with a title scene and full camera/light direction. Fold angles are the assumed values in `app/src/scene/postures.ts` (`open` 180°, `book` 122°, `seated` 112°, `standing` 62°, `closed` 0°). Camera positions are offsets from the device center in the Stage's world units (device root scaled ×0.001 from mm, per `Stage.ts`); focal length is expressed as the `PerspectiveCamera` vertical FOV, following the app's existing narrow-lens default of 22°.

**0. Title.** *Posture:* Closed (0°), held. *Camera:* FOV 18° (tighter than the app default, for a poster-like compression), close, dead-on to the outer display, camera fixed. *Screen:* the outer display fades up from black to the current time. *Copy:* headline "The hinge is the attention dial." — no body. *Background/light:* pure black, single key light rising from 0 to full over the fade (our choice, ties to "materialize" grammar used for Liquid Glass elsewhere in the design system). *Transition in:* cut from black. *Transition out:* the key light warms from black to dawn blue as scene 1 begins — no cut. *Driven by:* time (a held beat, ~1.5s, before scroll unlocks).

**1. Night to morning, Standing.** *Posture:* Standing (62°), resting. *Camera:* FOV 24° (a touch wider, to let the tent's silhouette read), three-quarter low angle (camera near ground level, looking slightly up), static but for a very slow 4°/min drift — the only scene allowed idle motion, since this is the ambient posture. *Screen:* the bedside clock face, red-tinted (hub-brief: "A red tint at night," T9/B4/B5/B7); it cross-fades to full color as the light warms. *Copy:* kicker "Standing" / headline "Quiet, until it isn't." / body "An overnight task just finished." *Background/light:* near-black warming through deep blue to a pale dawn gradient (our choice; the only scene with a light *ramp* rather than a cut, since it depicts dawn). *Transition in:* continues scene 0's warm-up. *Transition out:* camera dollies in and down as the device lifts (hover increases) into scene 2 — device motion carries the cut. *Driven by:* scroll, mapped to the dawn's color ramp (0→1 over the scene's scroll length).

**2. Picking it up, Closed.** *Posture:* Closed (0°), lifted (hover per `postures.ts`, ~34mm). *Camera:* FOV 20°, eye-level, dead-on to the outer display, a small handheld-feeling settle (2–3° of parallax as it arrives, damped by `spring.glance`). *Screen:* "Now and next" — "7 held · next digest 9:00," one message with an inline reply affordance. *Copy:* kicker "Closed" / headline "A glance is enough." / body "Reply, snooze, or let it wait." *Background/light:* flat, bright, neutral daylight gray-white (`rgb(245,245,247)`-family, measured Apple background-alt) — the calmest, most Apple-catalog-like frame in the piece, because this beat is deliberately small. *Transition in:* device motion (dolly-in from scene 1 completes here). *Transition out:* a hard cut to the unfold — the hinge itself performs the transition. *Driven by:* scroll for arrival; the reply tap (if taken) is a time-driven micro-beat.

**3. First unfold, Open.** *Posture:* Open (180°), lifted. *Camera:* FOV 22° (app default), pulls back and rises slightly as the hinge opens, ending three-quarter, level with the hinge line, distance increased ~15% versus scene 2 to fit the wider inner display. *Screen:* Today (main pane) beside the agent's proposed plan (supporting pane); one Approve, one Change. *Copy:* kicker "Open" / headline "Plan the day." / body "The agent proposes; you decide." *Background/light:* the daylight from scene 2 continues but brightens 10–15% and a soft rim light appears along the hinge edge (marks the "work" register — brighter, slightly more directional than the glance). *Transition in:* the fold drives it — `spring.hinge` on the device, camera dolly is a *reaction* to the fold, not simultaneous with it (principle 2: one mover per beat, staged a beat apart). *Transition out:* cut, hold on the resting open pose. *Driven by:* scroll opens the hinge (theta mapped to scroll progress); Approve/Change is a click, out of scroll.

**4. During the day, Closed.** *Posture:* Closed (0°), lifted, slight tilt (device held at a more casual angle than scene 2 — +8° yaw — since this is a snatched moment, not the first pickup). *Camera:* FOV 20°, slightly overhead (as if glanced at on a desk), quick settle. *Screen:* a Live Activity showing the agent's running task, and a Touch ID approval sheet. *Copy:* kicker "Closed" / headline "Working, in the background." / body "One thing needs your touch." *Background/light:* office daylight, cooler and flatter than scene 3 (a desk, not a plan) — distinct enough from scene 2's morning light that the two "Closed" scenes don't read as the same frame reused. *Transition in:* cut (this is a new moment in the day, not a continuous camera move — signal the jump with a cut, not a dolly). *Transition out:* cut to the Book scene. *Driven by:* scroll for arrival; the Live Activity's own progress is time-driven underneath.

**5. A research moment, Book.** *Posture:* Book (122°), lifted, held in a hands-width crop (frame is tighter — the device nearly fills the frame, since this posture is about intimacy). *Camera:* FOV 24°, looking into the fold from slightly above, so both halves are legible — this is the one scene where the fold band itself is the subject. *Screen:* a note on one half, its source on the other. *Copy:* kicker "Book" / headline "A note, and where it came from." / body — omit; let the two panes carry it. *Background/light:* warm, close, slightly vignetted (our choice — the most intimate light in the piece, reading as held-in-the-hands rather than set down). *Transition in:* cut. *Transition out:* cut, wider, to reveal the kitchen counter. *Driven by:* scroll; this is the one scene where a brief hands-off drag hint is offered (principle 6) before the story continues.

**6. Evening, Seated on the counter.** *Posture:* Seated (112°), resting. *Camera:* FOV 26° (widest in the piece, to include a suggestion of the counter surface — a shallow depth-of-field keeps the surroundings soft so the device stays the subject), slightly elevated three-quarter. *Screen:* top half a recipe from Memory; bottom half a timer, lights, and music. *Copy:* kicker "Seated" / headline "A station, not a screen." / body "Recipe up top. Controls within reach." *Background/light:* warm kitchen light, golden-hour orange key, the darkest non-night background besides scene 0 and 8 (our choice, evening register). *Transition in:* cut. *Transition out:* camera pulls back and levels as the device is picked up and opened — device motion (closing the fold partway, then reopening to Open) carries it. *Driven by:* scroll.

**7. Shutdown, Open.** *Posture:* Open (180°), lifted. *Camera:* FOV 22° (app default — this scene returns to the "default" camera as a formal echo of scene 3, since both are Open/planning beats). *Screen:* a short review of the day, the agent's ledger, tomorrow's first block. *Copy:* kicker "Open" / headline "A short review, then rest." / body "Tomorrow's first block is already set." *Background/light:* daylight cools toward dusk (a controlled version of scene 1's ramp, in reverse and compressed). *Transition in:* cut. *Transition out:* the hinge closes (`spring.hinge`, reversed) as the light drops toward night — device motion carries it, mirroring scene 3's opening. *Driven by:* scroll for arrival; the light ramp under it is time-driven once triggered.

**8. Night, Standing.** *Posture:* Standing (62°), resting. *Camera:* returns to scene 1's exact framing (FOV 24°, same low three-quarter angle) — a bookend, so the loop is legible as a loop. *Screen:* the calm red-tinted clock face. *Copy:* none, or a single word — "Quiet." — earning its silence after eight scenes of copy. *Background/light:* near-black, matching scene 1's start state exactly. *Transition in:* cut. *Transition out:* holds; a restart affordance (small, glass, bottom-of-frame) offers to loop to scene 0. *Driven by:* scroll to arrive; then time-driven (idle) until the person acts.

## Checklist: what "premium" means here

Testable against a screenshot or a short screen capture of each scene:

- The device fills 50–70% of the frame's short axis at rest (principle 1). A screenshot where it reads as "small in a big empty field" fails.
- No two adjacent scenes share both background color and key-light direction (principle 3).
- No debug control (slider, labeled button, posture switcher) is visible anywhere in the journey (Design direction).
- Exactly one thing moves per beat: either the device (a fold, lift, or set-down, on `spring.hinge`) or the camera (a dolly, arc, or rack focus) — never a scene where both move at once outside the paired "device leads, camera follows a beat later" pattern in scenes 3 and 7.
- Every scene has a kicker + headline ≤ 6 words, in the `presentation.text` scale — never the in-device Dynamic Type scale.
- Body copy, where present, is one sentence and appears only once the device/camera has settled — never overlapping active motion.
- A contact shadow is visible and grounded in every resting posture (Standing, Seated); floating postures (Closed, Open, Book while held) look intentionally lifted, not like a bug.
- Ambient scenes (0, 1, 8) have no looping motion beyond the one permitted slow drift in scene 1 (principle 5, hub-brief's "calm surfaces" rule).
- An interaction hint (drag cursor or small rotate arrow) appears only in scene 5's explore beat, and disappears after first use — nowhere else in the journey.
- No stacked post-processing (bloom + grain + aberration together); if depth of field is used, it is the only extra effect in that shot (principle 7).
- Frame rate holds during every transition, checked in Chrome DevTools' performance panel — a dropped-frame streak during a hinge or camera move fails the scene regardless of how it looks static.
