# Liquid Glass: Apple's own specification

Checked: September 23, 2026. Scope: what Apple itself says Liquid Glass *is* and *does* — optics, adaptivity, API surface, motion, shape, and accessibility — from Apple's HIG, developer documentation, and WWDC session transcripts, plus Apple's own support page and WWDC26 transcripts for post-launch changes. Does not cover how to *render* it (shaders, blur kernels, WebGL/Three.js technique) — that is the parallel rendering-research track. Raw downloads, JSON, and transcripts are in `.references/apple-web/docs/` (ignored by git, per project rules).

## Short answer

Liquid Glass is not blur+fill+border. Apple's own description is a "digital meta-material that dynamically bends and shapes light" ([WWDC25 219](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/)), built from several coupled layers — lensing/refraction, adaptive light/dark tone, adaptive shadow opacity, a highlights layer driven by a simulated environment and device motion, and a touch-illumination layer — that Apple explicitly says are not independent effects: "The light play, the depth effects, the adaptive changes, they aren't isolated features. They are layers inside a sophisticated system" (WWDC25 219). Structurally, the spec forbids the easy shortcut: glass elements must share a rendering region (`GlassEffectContainer` / `UIGlassContainerEffect` / `NSGlassEffectContainerView`) because "glass can not sample other glass" ([WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)), so a single-pass blur-and-tint per element is documented as producing "inconsistent behavior," not just worse performance. Two variants exist — Regular (adaptive) and Clear (non-adaptive, needs a manual dimming layer) — and Apple says they "should never be mixed." As of iOS 26.1 (November 2025) people can choose a Clear or Tinted system-wide look, and WWDC26 (June 2026, for the 2027 OS releases) replaced that binary toggle with a continuous slider and retuned the material's diffusion, edge darkening, and specular highlights.

Apple's iPhone Duo HIG page (published September 9, 2026) does not add any Duo-specific optical or behavioral rule for Liquid Glass; it only extends the existing vertical-toolbar/tab-bar layout guidance to the foldable's two displays. No Apple source opened for this note uses the terms "glass rail" or "vertical glass bar" — that vocabulary was not found.

## 1. Optics

- **It is not simple blur.** Apple's own summary: "Liquid Glass is a material that blurs content behind it, reflects color and light of surrounding content, and reacts to touch and pointer interactions in real time." ([Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views))
- **Lensing is the defining optical behavior**, described as physically motivated but not a simulation of real glass: "rather than trying to simply recreate a material from the physical world, Liquid Glass is a new digital meta-material that dynamically bends and shapes light... Where as previous materials scattered light, this new set of materials dynamically bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time." (WWDC25 219)
- **Refraction/sampling has a hard technical rule**, stated identically in the SwiftUI and AppKit sessions: "The glass material reflects and refracts lights, picking colors from nearby content. This effect is achieved by sampling content from an area larger than itself. However, glass can not sample other glass, so having nearby glass elements in different containers will result in inconsistent behavior." (WWDC25 323; near-identical wording in [WWDC25 310](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/))
- **Blur/frost is real but secondary to lensing**, and is described for the Regular variant specifically: "The regular variant blurs and adjusts the luminosity of background content to maintain legibility of text and other foreground elements. Scroll edge effects further enhance legibility by blurring and reducing the opacity of background content." ([HIG Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials))
- **"Frostier" is explicitly the Reduce Transparency behavior, not the default look**: "Reduced Transparency, makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it." (WWDC25 219) — i.e., heavy frosting is an accessibility fallback, not the intended optical character.
- **Thickness/depth scale with element size**, with specific listed effects: "When glass flexes and morphs to larger sizes – like when presenting a menu from a toolbar button – its material characteristics change to simulate a thicker, more substantial material. It casts deeper, richer shadows, has more pronounced lensing and refraction effects, and a softer scattering of light." (WWDC25 219)
- **Specular highlights come from a simulated environment plus device motion**: "Liquid Glass lives inside an environment that behaves like the world around us. Light sources inside of this environment shine on the material producing highlights that respond to geometry... On interactions, such as locking and unlocking your phone, these lights move in space, causing light to travel around the material, defining its silhouette. And in some cases, the lighting responds to device motion, making it feel like Liquid Glass is aware of its position in the real world." (WWDC25 219)
- **Touch illuminates the material from within**, and spreads to neighboring glass: "When you interact with Liquid Glass, the material illuminates from within as a form of feedback. Starting right under your fingertips, the glow spreads throughout the element and onto any Liquid Glass elements nearby." (WWDC25 219)
- **Content light "spills" onto glass, including into shadow**, on iPad/Mac's larger surfaces: "Light from colorful content nearby can subtly spill onto its surface, reinforcing the material's context and its sense of elevation within the interface. And the effect isn't limited to the surface, the light reflects, scatters, and bleeds into the shadow as well – much like it would in the physical world." (WWDC25 219)
- **Edge treatment was strengthened in 2026**: "we also introduced a darkened edge along with brighter specular highlights" to "establish more depth and separation," alongside better diffusion of "complex content behind it." ([WWDC26 102, Platforms State of the Union](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/102/))

## 2. Adaptivity

- **Shadows respond to what's underneath, per element**: "The element is aware of what's behind it and increases the opacity of its shadow when it is over text. Conversely, it lowers the opacity of its shadow when it is over a solid light background." (WWDC25 219) Also: "As text scrolls underneath, shadows become more prominent to create additional separation." (WWDC25 219)
- **Light/dark switching is size-gated, categorically not numerically**: "Small elements like navbars and tabbars, constantly adapt their appearance depending on what's behind them. They also flip from light to dark based on the background... Bigger elements, like menus or sidebars also adapt based on context, but they don't flip from light to dark. Their surface area is too big and transitions like these would be distracting." (WWDC25 219). HIG Color states the same split by named component category rather than a size number: "For smaller elements like toolbars and tab bars, the system can adapt Liquid Glass between a light and dark appearance in response to the underlying content... Liquid Glass appears more opaque in larger elements like sidebars to preserve legibility over complex backgrounds." ([HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)) **No numeric point-size or area threshold for "small" vs. "large" is published anywhere in the sources checked.**
- **Tint is content-derived by default, not a fixed color**: "By default, Liquid Glass has no inherent color, and instead takes on colors from the content directly behind it." (HIG Color) When a tint is applied: "Selecting a color generates a range of tones that are mapped to content brightness underneath the tinted element. It draws inspiration from how colored glass works in reality: changing its hue, brightness and saturation depending on what's behind without deviating too much from the intended color." (WWDC25 219)
- **The dimming layer for Clear glass has a published number**: "If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of **35% opacity**... If the underlying content is sufficiently dark, or if you use standard media playback controls from AVKit that provide their own dimming layer, you don't need to apply a dimming layer." (HIG Materials)
- **Legibility rule for symbols/text on Regular glass mirrors the glass's own flip**: "To maintain legibility, symbols and glyphs on top of Liquid Glass... flip from light to dark and vice versa, mirroring the glass's behavior to maximize contrast. All content placed on the Regular variant will automatically receive this treatment." (WWDC25 219)
- **Window/focus state changes appearance**: "when a window loses focus on the Mac or iPad, Liquid Glass shifts its appearance and visually recedes to guide attention." (WWDC25 219)
- **Regular vs. Clear conditions are explicit and exclusive**: Clear should only be used when "First, the element you're applying it to is over media-rich content. Second, your content layer won't be negatively affected by introducing a dimming layer. And lastly, the content sitting above it is bold and bright." Regular "works in any size, over any content and anything can be placed on top of it... Clear... does not have adaptive behaviors." (WWDC25 219)

## 3. Variants and API

**`Glass` (SwiftUI struct)** — [doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glass). Type properties/methods:

| Member | Meaning | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.regular` | "The regular variant of the Liquid Glass material." Adaptive; used automatically by `glassEffect()` | Default variant when none specified |
| `.clear` | "The clear variant of glass," permanently translucent, non-adaptive; HIG: needs a dimming layer for legibility | — |
| `.identity` | "Your content remains unaffected as if no glass effect was applied" | — |
| `.tint(_ color: Color?)` | "Returns a copy of the structure with a configured tint color" | `nil` (no tint) |
| `.interactive(_ isEnabled: Bool = true)` | "Returns a copy of the structure configured to be interactive" | `true` when called; effect absent unless called |

**`glassEffect(_:in:)` (View modifier)** — `func glassEffect(_ glass: Glass = .regular, in shape: some Shape = DefaultGlassEffectShape()) -> some View`. Renders a shape anchored to the view's bounds with the glass material, plus foreground effects. Default shape is a Capsule via `DefaultGlassEffectShape`. ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:)))

**`GlassEffectContainer`** — `init(spacing: CGFloat? = nil, content: () -> Content)`. "Combines multiple Liquid Glass shapes into a single shape that can morph individual shapes into one another." Spacing controls how near shapes must be before their paths blend: "The higher the spacing, the sooner blending begins as the shapes approach each other." **Default is `nil`; no numeric system default is published.** ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffectcontainer))

**`glassEffectID(_:in:)`** — `func glassEffectID(_ id: (some Hashable & Sendable)?, in namespace: Namespace.ID) -> some View`. Associates an identity so SwiftUI can animate matching shapes across appearance/disappearance. ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffectid(_:in:)))

**`glassEffectUnion(id:namespace:)`** — combines the geometries of multiple views with the same shape/variant/id into one rendered shape, even at rest, useful for dynamically generated views. ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffectunion(id:namespace:)))

**`glassEffectTransition(_:)` / `GlassEffectTransition`** — three cases: `.identity` ("no changes"), `.matchedGeometry` (default transition inside a container's spacing — geometry-matched morph), `.materialize` ("fade in content and animate in or out the glass material but will not attempt to match the geometry of any other glass effects" — for effects farther apart than the container's spacing). ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition))

**Button styles** — `.glass` (`GlassButtonStyle`, via `PrimitiveButtonStyle.glass`): "applies a Liquid Glass effect based on the button's context." `.glassProminent` (`GlassProminentButtonStyle`): "applies a prominent Liquid Glass effect... similar to the borderedProminent style." Both apply via `.buttonStyle(_:)`. ([glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/primitivebuttonstyle/glass), [glassProminent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/primitivebuttonstyle/glassprominent))

**UIKit — `UIGlassEffect`** (class): `init(style: UIGlassEffect.Style)`, `Style` enum has only `.regular` ("Standard glass effect style") and `.clear` ("Clear glass effect style") — no `.identity` or separate tint case at the UIKit layer. Properties: `isInteractive: Bool` ("Enables interactive behavior for the glass effect"), `tintColor: UIColor?` ("A tint color applied to the glass"). ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiglasseffect))

**UIKit — `UIGlassContainerEffect`** (class): property `spacing: CGFloat` — "specifies the distance between elements at which they begin to merge." No documented default value. Used with a `UIVisualEffectView` hosting nested `UIVisualEffectView`s configured with `UIGlassEffect`. ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiglasscontainereffect))

**AppKit** — `NSGlassEffectView` (content-view based, ties geometry to its `contentView` via Auto Layout; has corner radius and tint color properties) and `NSGlassEffectContainerView` (has a `spacing` property; groups glass elements for shared sampling and shared adaptive appearance). (WWDC25 310 — no dedicated reference JSON opened for these two classes; described from the session transcript only.)

## 4. Motion and interaction

- **Press feedback is scale + bounce + shimmer**, tied explicitly to the `interactive()` modifier: "Glass reacts to user interaction by scaling, bouncing, and shimmering, matching the effect provided by toolbar buttons and sliders." ([WWDC25 323](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/)) UIKit: "When tapping the button, it scales and bounces. To get that same kind of interactivity in your custom views, set `isInteractive` to true on the glassEffect." ([WWDC25 284](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/))
- **Illumination radiates from the touch point** and propagates to nearby glass, per the optics quote in §1 (WWDC25 219).
- **Materialize/dematerialize is not a fade** — it modulates the optical behavior itself: "Instead of fading, Liquid Glass objects materialize in and out by gradually modulating the light bending and lensing, ensuring a graceful transition that preserves the optical integrity of the material." (WWDC25 219) UIKit: "Glass appears using a special materialize animation... Always prefer setting the effect property over the alpha to ensure that the glass dematerializes or materializes with the appropriate animation." (WWDC25 284)
- **Morphing between shapes is geometry-driven**, governed by container spacing: "Customize the spacing on the container to control how the Liquid Glass effects behind views interact with one another. The larger the spacing value on the container, the sooner the Liquid Glass effects behind views blend together and merge the shapes during a transition." ([Applying Liquid Glass to custom views](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views)) In UIKit: "As long as there is space between them, they appear as two separate views. Only if they get closer, they start merging like small droplets of water." (WWDC25 284)
- **New in AppKit for 2026**: an interactive "bounce when clicked" response on custom glass, described but not given an API name in the transcript: "there is an effect that can be added to glass. Where the glass subtly bounces when clicked, giving a sense that the control is responding to interaction... use this effect with controls and buttons, or glass containers of interactive controls. A little goes a long way!" ([WWDC26 289, Modernize your AppKit app](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/289/))
- **Scroll edge effect has two named styles**, soft (default) and hard, and is explicitly not decorative: "Scroll edge effects... don't block or darken like overlays. They simply clarify where UI and content meet." ([WWDC25 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)) Behavior detail: "As content begins to scroll underneath a glass element, the effect gently dissolves the content into the background, lifting the glass visually above the moving content... When darker content scrolls under, triggering the glass itself to transition to its dark style, the effect intelligently switches to apply a subtle dimming instead." Hard style is for "pinned accessory views under a toolbar, such as column headers," applied "uniformly across the height of the toolbar," not gradually. (WWDC25 219)
- **Reduce Motion strips the elasticity, not just speed**: "Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material." (WWDC25 219)

## 5. Shape and layout

- **Three shape types define concentricity**: "fixed shapes have a constant corner radius. Capsules use a radius that's half the height of the container. And concentric shapes calculate their radius by subtracting padding from the parent's." (WWDC25 356) Capsule is the default `glassEffect` shape and the reason: "The capsule's geometry naturally supports concentricity... you'll see it throughout the system." (WWDC25 356)
- **Liquid Glass is a distinct navigation layer above content, not a content-layer material**: "Liquid Glass forms a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements — like tab bars and sidebars — that floats above the content layer... Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." (HIG Materials) The one named exception: transient interactive elements like sliders/toggles "take on a Liquid Glass appearance ... when a person activates it." (HIG Materials)
- **Glass-on-glass is explicitly disallowed**: "always avoid glass on glass. Stacking Liquid Glass elements on top of each other can quickly make the interface feel cluttered and confusing. When placing elements on top of Liquid Glass, avoid applying the material to both layers." (WWDC25 219)
- **iPhone Duo**: controls (toolbar, tab bar, navigation, plus system elements like the Dynamic Island and status bar) move to a vertical axis on the outer display and on the inner display in landscape; the inner display in portrait keeps horizontal bars. No Duo-specific Liquid Glass optical rule is stated; the guidance is entirely about placement order, grouping, and overflow priority, e.g. "Reserve the top of the vertical axis for primary navigation controls... followed by prominent actions... Items overflow from bottom to top by default." ([HIG Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)) This page's own changelog marks it as new on September 9, 2026, with no later revision.

## 6. Accessibility

Apple names exactly three accessibility settings that modify Liquid Glass, all quoted from a single passage: "Reduced Transparency, makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it. Increased contrast, makes elements predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border and Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material. These are available automatically whenever you use the new material." (WWDC25 219)

- **Reduce Transparency** → more frost, more obscuring of background content (i.e., pushes toward the "cheap trick" look, but only as a fallback, not the default).
- **Increase Contrast** → elements become "predominantly black or white" with "a contrasting border" — notably, a border only appears here, under this accessibility mode, not in the default look.
- **Reduce Motion** → reduced effect intensity and no "elastic properties."
- HIG Materials repeats that variant appearance itself "can differ in response to certain system settings, like if people choose a preferred look for Liquid Glass in their device's settings, or turn on accessibility settings that reduce transparency or increase contrast in the interface" (HIG Materials) — folding the user-facing Clear/Tinted preference (§7) into the same adaptivity mechanism as the accessibility settings.
- Third-party, clearly labeled (Engadget, "How to adjust the Liquid Glass effect in iOS 26.1"): "you won't be able to choose one of the two Liquid Glass options if you have the Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast settings enabled... Apple instructs you to turn off those features before you can customize the Liquid Glass setting." Not verified against an Apple primary source in this pass.

## 7. Changes since WWDC25

- **iOS/iPadOS/macOS 26.1 (November 3, 2025, per third-party reporting — MacRumors, 9to5Mac, Engadget)**: Apple's own support page confirms the substance: "Liquid Glass setting gives you the option to choose between the default clear look or a new tinted look which increases opacity of the material in apps and notifications on the Lock Screen." ([Apple Support, About iOS 26 Updates](https://support.apple.com/en-us/123075)) Third-party sources place the control at Settings → Display & Brightness (System Settings → Appearance on Mac) and note it is a binary Clear/Tinted choice, not a continuous slider, at this release.
- **WWDC26 / iOS, iPadOS, macOS 27 (June 2026, for "2027 releases")** — from Apple's own session transcripts:
  - Diffusion and depth: "we tuned Liquid Glass so it more effectively diffuses complex content behind it... we also introduced a darkened edge along with brighter specular highlights." (WWDC26 102)
  - The toggle became a slider: "a new slider in settings to adjust Liquid Glass anywhere from ultra clear to fully tinted, allowing users to choose the look that works best for them." (WWDC26 102)
  - Automatic adoption: "Apps already using Liquid Glass get these improvements automatically when they run on this year's releases without even needing to recompile." (WWDC26 102)
  - Compatibility opt-out removed: "we'll be removing support for opting to use the old design. So once your app is recompiled with Xcode 27, it will automatically begin to use the new design with Liquid Glass." (WWDC26 102) — this retires the `UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` escape hatch described in the original [Adopting Liquid Glass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass) guide.
  - Sidebars/windows (Mac/iPad): "Sidebars expand to the edges on Mac and iPad, providing clearer structure while still refracting content from your app and the wallpaper. And icons in the sidebar regain their color using your app's accent color... every window on macOS now also has the same tighter corner radius." (WWDC26 102)
  - New interactive "bounce when clicked" for custom AppKit glass (§4, WWDC26 289).
  - Icon-specific: "we've introduced new features such as refraction that can be selectively used for added character" in app icon layers, previewable in an updated Icon Composer (WWDC26 102).
- **Third-party summary of the same period** (MacRumors, "Here's How Liquid Glass Is Changing in iOS 27," and Wikipedia's Liquid Glass article, both clearly labeled as non-Apple): frames the WWDC26 pass as a deliberate walk-back after "nine months of documented usability criticism," reducing default transparency and improving readability; none of this framing is Apple's own language, only the technical description above is.
- **iPhone Duo**: no Apple source opened mentions Liquid Glass changes specific to Duo beyond the general vertical-control layout in the September 9, 2026 HIG page (§5). No WWDC26 session found in this pass discusses Duo and Liquid Glass together.

## 8. Numbers

Published, verbatim:

- Clear-glass dimming layer: **35% opacity**, dark, applied "if the underlying content is bright" (HIG Materials, exact figure).
- `Glass.interactive(_ isEnabled: Bool = true)` — default `true` when the method is called at all.
- `GlassEffectContainer(spacing: CGFloat? = nil, ...)` — default parameter is `nil`; **no numeric system default spacing is published**.
- `UIGlassContainerEffect.spacing: CGFloat` — settable, but **no documented default value** found.
- `UIGlassEffect.Style` — exactly two cases (`.regular`, `.clear`); no numeric parameters.
- General (not Liquid-Glass-specific) WCAG AA contrast minimums Apple cites for its own Accessibility Inspector: up to 17pt text at any weight needs **4.5:1**; 18pt (any weight) or bold text at any size needs **3:1**. ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)) This is a general legibility standard, not a Liquid Glass parameter.

Not published, stated plainly:

- No numeric corner-radius values for the default Capsule/rect glass shapes. Code samples in Apple's own docs use values like `cornerRadius: 16.0` or container `spacing: 40.0` / `20.0` / `10.0`, but these are illustrative example values inside sample code, not documented defaults — Apple does not label them as system constants.
- No animation duration or spring-parameter numbers (stiffness, damping, response) for materialize, matchedGeometry morphing, or the press/bounce feedback are published in any source opened.
- No numeric intensity value for specular highlights, the "darkened edge" (WWDC26), or the touch-illumination glow radius.
- No numeric point-size or area threshold separating "small" (light/dark-flipping) from "large" (non-flipping) Liquid Glass elements — only the named-category examples (toolbars/tab bars vs. menus/sidebars).
- **My own estimate, explicitly labeled as such and not to be treated as Apple's value:** none offered in this note — where Apple did not publish a number, this note leaves it blank rather than guessing, per the task's instruction.

## Implementation requirements

1. Liquid Glass refracts and bends the content beneath it — bending/concentrating light, not merely blurring it — and this lensing is the material's primary visual identity, distinct from and in addition to blur.
2. Glass samples a region larger than its own bounds; two nearby glass elements rendered independently will sample each other incorrectly, so any faithful implementation must render mutually-adjacent glass shapes through one shared sampling pass (the `GlassEffectContainer`/`UIGlassContainerEffect` equivalent), not per-element.
3. The Regular variant is adaptive (shadow opacity, light/dark tone, tint blending with background) and works over any content; the Clear variant is non-adaptive, permanently more transparent, and requires a manual ~35%-opacity dark dimming layer when placed over bright content — the two must not be visually interchangeable or mixed in the same UI.
4. Small floating elements (toolbars, tab bars) flip their whole appearance between light and dark based on what's behind them; large elements (sidebars, menus, expanded glass) stay more opaque and do not flip, instead adapting shadow depth and lensing strength.
5. Tint has no fixed color by default — it is generated from the color/brightness of the content behind the element, changing hue/brightness/saturation while keeping the intended hue recognizable, like real stained glass, not a flat semi-transparent color layer.
6. Shadows cast by glass elements change opacity based on what's directly behind them (deeper over text, lighter over a plain light background) — shadow is content-aware, not a fixed drop-shadow value.
7. Highlights come from a simulated light environment and shift with device motion (gyroscope) and with UI events like lock/unlock, not just from a static gradient.
8. Touch interaction illuminates the glass from the contact point outward and can spread to adjacent glass elements in the same container; this is a distinct feedback layer from scale/bounce.
9. Interactive glass elements scale, bounce, and shimmer on press; this is opt-in per element (`interactive()` / `isInteractive`) rather than automatic for every glass surface.
10. Elements must not fade to appear/disappear — they "materialize"/"dematerialize" by modulating lensing and light-bending strength; shape changes (e.g., button-to-menu) morph via matched geometry within a shared container, gated by the container's spacing value, or via a "materialize" transition when the shapes are unrelated or far apart.
11. Liquid Glass belongs to a single floating navigation/control layer above content; it must not be applied within the content layer (except transient interactive controls like an activated slider/toggle knob) and must never be stacked glass-on-glass.
12. Scroll content passing under glass gets a scroll edge effect (soft by default, hard for dense/pinned UI) that fades/blurs/dims the content near the edge — this is a distinct, named system behavior, not a generic overlay darkening.
13. Reduce Transparency increases frost/opacity of glass; Increase Contrast forces near-black/white fills with a visible border (a border is otherwise not part of the default look); Reduce Motion removes elastic/spring behavior and reduces effect intensity — all three must be supported as explicit, distinct modes.
14. As of iOS/macOS 26.1, a system-wide Clear/Tinted preference exists and Liquid Glass must read and honor it; as of iOS/macOS 27 (2026 WWDC cycle) that preference becomes a continuous ultra-clear-to-fully-tinted slider, and the default rendering also gained a darkened edge and brighter specular highlights plus improved diffusion over busy backgrounds.
15. Apple has not published a Liquid Glass-specific vocabulary or ruleset for iPhone Duo beyond routing standard toolbar/tab-bar/navigation controls to a vertical axis on the outer display and inner-landscape display — there is no documented "glass rail" concept to implement against.
