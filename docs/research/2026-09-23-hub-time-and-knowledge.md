# Hub precedents: time, attention, and personal knowledge

Checked: September 23, 2026. Scope: precedents and research for two hub areas: **time and attention**, and **personal knowledge**. The note also covers reimagined home screens and hubs. It asks what makes a hub feel centralized without becoming a cluttered dashboard. It builds on the [signal audit](2026-09-23-signal-audit.md), [source index](2026-09-23-source-index.md), and [Apple guidance note](2026-09-23-apple-guidance.md), and does not restate the Duo layout, reserved-region, or vertical-control rules covered there. No prototype was built.

**Evidence tiers:** **Vendor** = official vendor publication; **Firsthand** = firsthand report or review; **Research** = research study; **Opinion** = designer or practitioner opinion. **(SR)** means the claim comes from search-result text; the page was not opened or did not render. The mapping to device states (outer display, inner display, tabletop) is inference. None of it has been tested on a Duo.

## Short answer

The precedents that feel **centralized** combine many sources into one or two underlying structures, then show only a slice chosen by relevance. The ones that feel **cluttered** keep many always-on tiles or cards on one surface. For the Duo hub:

1. **Centralize the underlying data, not the screen.** Build two spines: **Today** (one timeline of tasks and events) and **Memory** (one personal index, browsed by time, people, and meaning). Every module is a view onto one of them. Planning tools (T1) and the Stuff I've Seen study (K1) both point this way.
2. **Opening the device should reveal more depth about the same item, not more items.** Apple's widget and Live Activity guidance scales the same content with size, and says not to fill extra space with more content (T7, T8). This matches the Duo HIG's continuity rule.
3. **Batch interruptions and hold them until a natural break.** Three batches a day beat both the usual stream and no notifications at all. Delivering at breakpoints cut cognitive load by 33–46% (T12). Opening or closing the hinge is a *candidate* breakpoint; this is untested.
4. **Use AI triage only if people can see and undo it.** Label AI output, show the originals, say why an item was ranked high, and allow dismissal in one gesture. Apple's own track record and 2025–26 reviews show how it fails: false headlines, poor ranking of third-party apps, stale personal context, invented details (T6, K8).
5. **Build around a few rituals, not settings.** Guided planning and shutdown flows work, while per-context setup is powerful but seldom done (T2, T4). The hub should be arranged around three moments: plan, focus, and shut down.
6. **Make capture one gesture, processing a scheduled step, and retrieval navigation-first.** People still prefer browsing to search for their own files (K2). Collecting without processing is a known trap (K7).
7. **The tabletop state has the weakest evidence.** StandBy gives a proven grammar for reading at a distance (T9). Foldable reviews, however, show the half-folded pose used mainly for video, calls, and selfies (H7). Keep the tabletop hub modest until it is tested.

| T10 | Notification volume is steady, not exploding, and messaging drives it. | Mean 63.5 per day (n=15, 1 week), mostly messengers and email, checked within minutes ([Pielot et al. 2014](https://pielot.org/2014/07/an-in-situ-study-of-mobile-phone-notifications-mobilehci-14/), Research). Follow-up: median 56 per day across 794,525 notifications from 278 users ([Pielot et al. 2018](https://pielot.org/2018/06/dismissed-a-detailed-exploration-of-how-mobile-phone-users-handle-push-notifications/), Research, SR). Disruption is highest mid-task and lowest before a new task or when idle; the sender relationship matters ([Mehrotra et al. 2016](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1502364/), Research, SR). | Triage must handle about 50–60 items a day. **People**, not apps, are the primary priority key. |
| T11 | Interruptions are costly even when ignored. | A notification alone disrupted an attention task ([Stothart et al. 2015](https://carystothart.com/publications/attentional-cost-cell-phone-notification/), Research, SR). A live interruption is the exception and must justify itself. |
| T12 | **Batching and breakpoints work; going silent backfires.** | RCT (n=237): three daily batches made people "more attentive, productive, in a better mood, and in greater control." With 24 h of no notifications (n=30), people felt more productive but anxious about responsiveness ([Pielot & Rello](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02314), Research). Breakpoint delivery on phones cut cognitive load 46% in the lab and 33% in the wild (30 people, 16 days) ([Attelia](https://keio.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/attelia-reducing-users-cognitive-load-due-to-interruptive-notific), Research). The desktop system OASIS reduced frustration ([Iqbal & Bailey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/1879831.1879833), Research, SR). | Plan for about three scheduled digests a day, with breakthrough for key people, delivered at breakpoints. Do not make zero notifications the default. Hypothesis to test: unfolding is a breakpoint, so the digest can wait for the inner display. |

## 2. Personal knowledge

| # | Finding | Evidence (tier) | Implication for the hub |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K1 | One index of "stuff I've seen" beat separate tools. | In an internal deployment with more than 230 people, users found things more easily, used "other search tools less frequently," and relied on "time and people" as cues. They re-sorted by **date** more than by rank ([Dumais et al. 2003](https://sigir.org/files/forum/2015D/p028.pdf), Research). | The **Memory spine** is one index across notes, mail, files, the web, and events. It sorts by time by default, filters by person, and links to Today. |
| K2 | People still navigate more than they search their own material. | Users estimated 56–68% of file retrievals were by navigation and 4–15% by search, whatever the search engine ([Bergman et al. 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1402256.1402259), Research, SR). People "orienteer" in small, context-guided steps rather than jumping straight to the target by keyword ([Teevan et al. 2004](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/perfect-search-engine-not-enough-study-orienteering-behavior-directed-search/), Research, SR). | Semantic search adds to browsable structure (project, day, person) but does not replace it. The **inner** display is where browsing happens. |
| K3 | Total capture is the wrong goal, and ambient recording faces backlash. | "Rather than try to capture everything, system design should focus on the psychological basis of human memory" ([Sellen & Whittaker 2010](https://cacm.acm.org/research/beyond-total-capture/), Research, SR). Microsoft Recall launched on by default with snapshots stored unencrypted. It became opt-in, encrypted, and Windows Hello-gated ([The Record](https://therecord.media/microsoft-reverses-course-recall-opt-in), Firsthand, SR), yet still draws security criticism in 2026 ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/one-year-after-its-rocky-launch-microsofts-windows-recall-still-raises-security-red-flags/), Firsthand, SR). Apple's Journaling Suggestions groups moments on-device, and apps receive only what the user picks ([Apple privacy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/journaling-suggestions/), Vendor, SR). | Capture is explicit. System suggestions are a picker the user approves, never a silent log. |
| K4 | One-gesture capture that follows through automatically is well received. | Nothing's Essential Key saves a screenshot with a press or a transcribed voice memo with a hold. The AI "extracts dates, attempts to create to-do lists" and sets reminders; the reviewer called it "one of the best integrations of AI" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/mobile/smartphones/nothing-phone-3a-essential-spaces-ai-powered-app-done-right-163042976.html), Firsthand). Its placement under the power button causes accidental presses ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/nothing-power-button-3530793/), Firsthand, SR). Raycast for iOS reaches capture through the Action button, widgets, Control Center, and its keyboard, but cannot replace Spotlight under iOS sandboxing ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/04/30/raycast-launches-ios-app-its-first-expansion-beyond-the-mac/), Firsthand, SR). iOS 27 adds natural-language "Describe an event in Calendar" ([About iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149076), Vendor). | **Outer** display plus a hardware gesture handle capture. The AI *proposes* the follow-through (a reminder or event), and the user confirms it at the next review. |
| K5 | AI-organized notes shift the work from filing to resurfacing. | Mem 2.0: "we built Mem to handle the organizing part for you." Deep Search works by meaning. "Heads Up" shows your history with a person before a meeting ([Mem, Oct 2025](https://get.mem.ai/blog/introducing-mem-2-0), Vendor). Mem 1.0 drew reliability complaints ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/mem-2/reviews), Firsthand, SR). Reflect Open (July 2026) offers AI chat with citations and keeps notes marked private away from AI ([Reflect](https://reflect.app/blog/reflect-open), Vendor, SR). | A "Heads Up"-style card tied to the next event is the best bridge between Today and Memory. AI answers cite their notes, and any note can be excluded from AI. |
| K6 | Resurfacing works best as a small, bounded daily ritual. | Readwise Daily Review picks highlights by probability, and "each time a highlight is shown, its probability of resurfacing is significantly decreased." Mastery cards use half-lives of 7, 14, or 28 days ([Readwise docs](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise/docs/faqs/reviewing-highlights), Vendor). | Put a short review inside the plan or shutdown ritual, not in an endless feed. |
| K7 | Collecting feels like progress but is not. | "'to know about something' isn't the same as 'knowing something'"; the fix is short cycles of processing ([Tietze 2014](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/collectors-fallacy/), Opinion). A study title reports bookmarks "are created but not used" ([Bergman et al. 2021](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0961000620949652), Research, SR; abstract not verified). PARA files by actionability (projects, areas, resources, archives) ([Forte](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/), Opinion). | Give the inbox a finite queue and a processing step. Group knowledge by active project, which links it to Today, rather than by a topic taxonomy. |
| K8 | Assistants that draw on personal context are still unreliable, and their failures are opaque. | iOS 27 Siri AI: "feels *amazing*" when it works, but questions with data in iMessage "couldn't be answered… or… the context about me was simply stale"; "a mysterious black box" ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-27-review/6/), Firsthand). Pixel Magic Cue appeared rarely and gave a wrong or invented address ([Android Police, Sep 2025](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-pixel-10magic-cue-suggestions-terrible/), Firsthand), then became useful about two months later ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/two-months-later-pixel-10-magic-cue-delivered-3610389/), Firsthand, SR). Human-AI guidelines: "Make it easy to dismiss or ignore undesired AI system services"; make clear why the system acted ([HAX G8](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/guideline/support-efficient-dismissal/); [Amershi et al. 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3290605.3300233), Research). | Show how fresh the index is and where each answer came from. Proactive cards must be rare, sourced, and dismissible in one gesture. |
| K9 | Apple Notes in iOS 27 is incremental (section links, Copy as Markdown, Siri AI creating and updating notes). | [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/30/heres-everything-new-for-apple-notes-in-ios-27/) (Firsthand, SR). | A single index across apps would be a **concept-level** departure; the platform does not offer it. |

## 4. Principles: centralized without clutter

| Principle | Evidence | Outer (glance, quick action) | Inner (workspace) | Tabletop (ambient) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Two spines, many views.** Unify the data, not the surface. | T1, K1, K5 | The now/next item from Today, plus a "Heads Up" card for the next person or event. | Timeline and inbox; Memory browsing by project, day, and person. | The next event and a clock. |
| **Depth, not breadth, on opening.** Opening reveals more of the item already in view. | T7, T8, H6; Duo HIG continuity ([Apple guidance](2026-09-23-apple-guidance.md)) | The short look: one line per item. | The long look: detail, sources, actions. | A scaled-up single item. |
| **Batch and defer.** Default to quiet, a few digests, and breakthrough only for key people. | T10–T12 | Breakthrough items and the count of held items. | The digest is reviewed here (a hypothesis: unfolding as the breakpoint). | Silent; urgent people only. |
| **Show and undo the AI.** Separate condense, rank, and hold; label them; open originals; explain; dismiss. | T5, T6, K8 | An italic or marked summary line; swipe to dismiss or correct. | "Why this is here," originals, source freshness. | No AI rewriting of text at a distance (conservative default). |
| **Rituals over settings.** Plan, focus, shut down; inferred contexts with one-tap override. | T2–T4, K6 | Context chip; a Live Activity for the focus block. | Plan and shutdown flows with a capacity check and a short review. | A focus timer as a Live Activity. |
| **Capture fast, process on schedule, retrieve by browsing first.** | K2, K4, K7 | One-gesture capture (voice, photo, text) with no filing. | Inbox processing; semantic search alongside project, day, and person browsing. | Voice capture only (to be tested). |
| **Calm by default, full power when open.** | H1–H4 | Text-first, low color, a short list. | Full capability and density. | StandBy grammar: large type, no backgrounds, red at night. |

## 5. Pitfalls and guardrails

| Pitfall | Evidence | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dashboard sprawl: many live modules competing. | H4, H5; Widgets HIG warns against app-like layouts (T7) | Cap the modules per state. Modules change only when their data does; animation lasts at most 2 s, per the HIG (T7, T8). |
| The system inserts things the user didn't add. | Smart Stack Widget Suggestions can insert widgets, and Apple exposes switches to turn it and Smart Rotate off (T7) | The hub may reorder but never add without consent. Pinning always wins. |
| An AI summary changes the meaning. | BBC case and the iOS 26 labeling (T6) | Label summaries, reveal originals in one tap, exclude safety- and time-critical items, and add a "report" action. |
| Triage favors first-party or high-volume apps. | Tech Nerdiness: Slack rarely prioritized (T6) | People-first ranking. Sources can declare urgency. The ranking reason is shown. |
| Stale or missing personal context. | MacStories, Magic Cue (K8) | Show freshness ("indexed 2 min ago"). Say "I can't see that yet" rather than guess. |
| Total capture and privacy backlash. | Recall (K3) | Explicit capture only. On-device indexing. Per-source and per-note exclusion. |
| The collector's inbox that grows forever. | K7 | Finite queue, a processing step in the ritual, and auto-archive of unprocessed items after N days (to be tested). |
| Muting everything, which raises anxiety and FoMO. | T12 | Offer batched modes, not "off." Always show the held count and the next digest time. |
| Too many contexts to configure. | T4 | Infer contexts from the calendar. Limit to two or three. The active context is always visible. |

## 6. Where the concept departs from platform limits

Mark these as **concept-only**. A third-party app can't do them today, based on the sources reviewed:

- **Reading and re-ranking all system notifications, and holding them for digests.** The platform does this through Apple Intelligence (T5); third-party apps receive only their own notifications.
- **A cross-app personal index** spanning Mail, Messages, files, and web history (K1). This is Siri AI or Spotlight territory in iOS 27.
- **Owning the outer display's home or lock surface.** Third parties get widgets and Live Activities, not the home layer (T7, T8).
- **A dedicated capture gesture** in the style of the Essential Key. The Action button can launch an app (K4), but the Duo's hardware controls are not assessed here.
- **Treating hinge events as notification breakpoints.** Hinge observation APIs exist ([source index](2026-09-23-source-index.md)), but delivering system notifications on a hinge event does not.

## 7. Open questions and limits

- **Is unfolding a good breakpoint?** Breakpoint research (T12) comes from desktop task structure and phone UI events, not hinge use. Test whether the digest-on-open pattern reduces load, or instead trains people to unfold compulsively.
- **Is tabletop an ambient station or an occasional pose?** StandBy precedent (T9) is strong, but foldable evidence (H7) suggests tabletop is used for video, calls, and timers. Duo StandBy behavior can't be simulated yet ([signal audit](2026-09-23-signal-audit.md)). Observe after October 23.
- **Where do Apple's controls end?** Whether third-party hub content can take part in iOS 27 prioritization, or guide it, is undocumented. A vendor blog says it can't be steered (T6); unverified with Apple.
- **Evidence gaps:** No controlled study of minimalist launchers (H3). No study of AI notification summaries in the wild; reviews are short and individual (T6). The Duo-specific rows (outer, inner, tabletop) are inference from other devices.
- **Source limits:** Several research claims come from search-result abstracts (marked SR) because ACM, PNAS, and SAGE pages returned 403 errors. The Fitz, Pielot & Rello, Stuff I've Seen, and Attelia findings were read in the source. The Apple Support and HIG pages were read in full through their page or JSON data. Vendor claims (Mem, Sunsama, Akiflow, Amie) describe intent, not measured outcomes. Pricing and plan details were not checked.
- **Not covered here:** the AI-agent and ambient/home areas (beyond T9 and H7), accessibility of glance surfaces, and multi-user or household contexts.
